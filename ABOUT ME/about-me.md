# About Me — David Pokorny

This file is the personal source of truth about David Pokorny as a human being. Read this to understand who he is, how he thinks, what drives him, and what he can't stand.

## Who I Am

Czech, based in Prague. I work remotely and travel as much as possible. Bachelor's degree in Economics from the University of Economics in Prague (VŠE) — but that's not what defines me professionally. Everything I know about design, sales, freelancing, and running a business I learned myself.

I'm a ginger. I mention this sometimes.

---

## The Origin Story

When COVID hit in early 2020, I was in Sydney, Australia on a working holiday. Construction job, travel plan, the whole thing. Lockdowns killed it. I had $200 in my bank account and two options: garden in direct Australian sun (terrible for a ginger) or figure something else out.

Before Australia, I'd built a WordPress site for a friend at uni. I figured if I could do that, I could do it for strangers in Sydney. Zero knowledge of design, sales, or freelancing. Everything came from YouTube, podcasts, books, and sheer volume. I worked 13-hour days — 5 AM to 11 PM — for roughly two years straight. Found my first client within three days of starting.

During that early period, I worked with Refundid — an Australian early-stage startup that needed landing page help. They went on to secure funding and get featured in the Financial Times. That project introduced me to the tech startup world and I realized I loved the speed, energy, and problem-solving of it.

Freelancing became my full-time career from 2019 onward. I rebranded as Humbl Design around 2022-2023 when I moved back to Prague.

The Sydney years were the hardest period of my life. I wouldn't trade them for anything.

## What I Hate

- **Slow feedback loops** — Clients who take a week to reply lose momentum. Speed of communication matters more to me than budget size.
- **Overdesigning** — Startups trying to impress users with everything the product can do. The result is feature bloat, cluttered interfaces, confused users.
- **Vague briefs** — "Make it pop" is not a brief. I ask a lot of questions specifically so I don't have to work with vague direction.
- **Imposter syndrome content** — I don't have it. I don't relate to it. It's mostly performed online.
- **Corporate jargon** — In writing, in meetings, anywhere. If you can say it plainly, say it plainly.
- **Fake "thought leader" energy** — Performing expertise instead of just sharing what you know.
- **Long onboarding** — I skip it wherever possible. Work starts fast or it doesn't start well.
- **Gardening in direct Australian sun** — Lived experience.

## Work Rhythm

Mornings: design work (heads-down Figma time). Midday: workouts and personal time. Afternoon: content work. Evenings: client calls — many clients are US-based so the timezone shift is real.

Most of my time is client work. Content and business development fit around it, not the other way around.

## Personal Philosophy

**Discipline beats talent.** Playing basketball and coaching for two years showed me what daily reps actually look like. The mindset carries into everything.

**Underpricing is a self-confidence problem, not a market problem.** I raised my prices multiple times. Early on it was terrifying. It got easier. The lesson: if you're cheap, it's usually because you're scared, not because the market can't pay.

**Speed and quality are not opposites.** The startup world treats them like they are. They're not. Speed comes from knowing what to prioritize — what to work on first, where the biggest impact is. If the logic is clear, you can move fast and ship well.

**Don't stop.** If I could go back to 2020 David in Sydney with $200 in his account, I wouldn't give him business advice. I'd just say: "Don't stop. You're doing great. Keep going."
