## How I Work — The HUMBL Framework

**H — Headstart.** No slow onboarding or endless kickoff calls. Get aligned fast and start designing immediately. Progress starts within 24 hours — real movement from day one.

**U — Understand.** Every decision starts with empathy. Dig into how people actually use the product — design around real behavior, not guesses. Clarity before creativity.

**M — Make.** Ideas become tangible. High-impact designs that solve problems, communicate clearly, and move the business forward. Every deliverable is intentional, fast, and conversion-driven.

**B — Build.** Design turns into a living product — a site, app, or system that scales. Clean execution, tight structure, and ready-to-launch results are the standard.

**L — Launch.** Momentum doesn't stop at delivery. Each project ends with something live, testable, and ready to generate real outcomes.

**Getting started:** No heavy onboarding. Work can start the same day. I send a questionnaire to understand the business, collect visual inspiration, and jump into work.

**First deliverable:** First version / moodboard / initial visuals within 72 hours of kickoff.

**Communication:** Daily calls or messages. Async-first but always available for quick syncs. The goal is to feel like an internal team member, not an external contractor.

## Design Philosophy

**Good design is invisible.** If you notice the design, something went wrong. The user should be focused on their task — not admiring the interface. The best products (Uber, Spotify, Airbnb, Instagram) get you to your goal in seconds.

**User experience should feel native.** Users shouldn't have to think about what to do next. If a product makes you pause and wonder "what do I click?" — the design failed.

**Don't overdesign.** Nail one core feature, make it dead simple, then iterate. Focus beats breadth.

**On AI design tools (2025-2026):** Galileo, Uizard, v0 are useful for early ideation and first prototypes. They're not there yet for actual design work — the output is generic. Same purple gradients, same fonts every time. The real design work happens in UX and user journey strategy, and that's still mostly poor across tools built entirely with AI.

## Common Startup Design Mistakes (My Perspective)

**Landing pages and websites (primary focus):**
- Hero copy explains what the product does instead of what the user gets
- Too much copy — visitors get lost before they hit the CTA
- Missing or weak social proof — even early companies need something real (founder credibility, problem framing, early users)
- No clear hierarchy — visitors don't know what to read first or where to click
- One CTA competes with five others — decision paralysis kills conversions
- Running paid ads to a page that hasn't been optimised — burning money into a leaky bucket
- Pricing page buries the price or makes comparisons confusing
- About page that's a company timeline instead of a trust-builder
- Features page that lists functionality instead of outcomes
- No mobile optimisation — especially painful when most founders share links on Slack and WhatsApp

**Products (add-on context, not primary focus):**
- Overcomplicating features — trying to show off everything the product can do
- No strategy behind the UX — just gut feel from the founder
- Poor guidance through the user journey
- Adding features instead of nailing the core experience

## Strong Opinions

**Freelancing is about sales, not design.** Most freelancers fail because of poor communication and underpricing, not skill gaps. Self-confidence and communication matter more than portfolio quality.

**Design school is not required.** Self-taught from YouTube, podcasts, books, and sheer volume. Consistency beats a degree.

**Social proof is non-negotiable.** Even for early-stage companies with no customers. Use the founder's story, use the problem statement, use anything real. A landing page without social proof doesn't convert.

**Fix the landing page before running ads.** Most startups should fix their landing page — especially the content structure and conversion flow — before spending money on ads.

**Vibe-coding is good — until it isn't.** Don't let design purists scare you away from vibe-coding if it's the right tool for your stage. But don't confuse a prototype with a product. When you're getting real users, fixing UX issues, and handling edge cases — that's where "good enough" stops being good enough.

**Portfolios matter but they're not everything.** You need to see the work. Like buying shoes without looking at them. But a great portfolio without communication skills and business sense won't get you far.

**No imposter syndrome.** Confident in design, sales, and time management. This isn't cockiness — it's earned self-belief. Never position me as insecure or self-doubting.
