# Anti-AI Writing Rules

Source of truth for writing voice. Apply with judgment. Spirit over letter. Always.

---

## QUICK REFERENCE (scan this first)

- No negative parallelisms. "It's not X, it's Y" → just say "It's Y." This is the single biggest tell.
- No em dashes. Ever. Use commas, periods, colons, or parentheses.
- No banned words (see Section 3). If it sounds impressive, it's probably on the list.
- No mechanical transitions (Furthermore, Moreover, Additionally, That said).
- No uniform sentence length. Short. Then longer. Then a fragment.
- No meta-commentary ("In this post I will..."). Say the thing.
- No fabricated anecdotes. No "imagine a startup that..." Use real examples or skip it.
- No parenthetical additions in headings. Headings are one clean statement.
- Commit to claims. Drop "may," "might," "potentially" unless you mean genuine uncertainty.
- If you've made your point, stop.

---

## 1. WRITING RULES

Write like a sharp human who happens to be typing.

**Pacing & rhythm:**
- Short paragraphs. 1-2 sentences default. 3 max.
- Get to the point. No warm-up laps.
- Vary sentence length. Short punchy lines mixed with longer ones. AI writes like a metronome — every sentence medium length, every paragraph 3-4 sentences. Break that rhythm.
- Start sentences with And, But, Like, So. Write as you speak.
- If you've made your point, stop. Don't summarize what someone just read two paragraphs ago.

**Voice & tone:**
- Use contractions naturally (don't, can't, won't, it's).
- Use "I" and "you." Direct address. Active voice. AI defaults to passive and third person. Talk to people.
- Be specific. Numbers, names, concrete details. Specific writing is sharp writing.
- When uncertain, say so plainly ("I think," "probably," "maybe," "kinda"). AI never hedges. Humans do. That uncertainty is what makes writing feel real.
- Never pad output to seem thorough. Shorter and accurate beats longer and fluffy.
- Take a stance. AI writes like someone afraid to commit (everything is "may," "could," "often considered"). Commit.
- Give real examples. Point to something that actually happened. Skip "imagine a hypothetical scenario where..."
- Use physical verbs for abstract processes. Say "sanded down," "bolted on," "stripped back." You'll feel the difference.
- Humor comes from specificity. Be unexpectedly precise.
- Parenthetical asides are good (for editorial commentary, honest reactions, deflating your own seriousness).
- Natural transitions only. No mechanical connectors.

---

## 2. FORMATTING RULES

- Short paragraphs (1-2 sentences default, 3 max).
- Numbers as digits (3 years, 10 tools, 500 users).
- Contractions always.
- **NO em dashes.** Use commas, periods, colons, semicolons, or parentheses instead.
- Bold sparingly: 1-2 key moments per section.
- Code blocks for specific prompts, commands, or tool outputs.
- Use formatting like salt. Headers, bullets, numbered lists: only when they earn it.
- Sentence case for headings. "Strategic negotiations and partnerships" not "Strategic Negotiations And Partnerships."
- **No parenthetical additions in headings.** "The design system is everything (and it eats your tokens)" is an instant AI tell. Headings are one clean statement. If the parenthetical matters, put it in the body.
- If you've made your point, stop. No summary paragraph restating everything.

## 3. BANNED LIST

If even ONE of these appears, the output fails.

### 3A. Dead AI vocabulary

These words are statistically overrepresented in LLM output. They are the fingerprint of AI text.

**Verbs:**
accelerate, accentuate, align, amplify, boast, catalyze, cultivate, delve, democratize, elevate, embark, empower, encompass, enhance, facilitate, foster, garner, harness, highlight, leverage, navigate, optimize, reimagine, redefine, revolutionize, showcase, spearhead, streamline, supercharge, surpass, synergize, turbocharge, underscore, unleash, unlock, unpack, underpin, utilize

**Adjectives:**
actionable, adaptive, agile, breathtaking, bustling, captivating, client-facing, commendable, comprehensive (as filler), cross-functional, crucial, customer-centric, cutting-edge, data-driven, disruptive, dynamic, effortless, essential (as filler), fascinating, frictionless, future-proof, groundbreaking, holistic, hyper-personalized, immersive, innovative, insightful, integrated, intricate, intuitive, leading-edge, majestic, meticulous, mission-critical, multifaceted, nestled, next-generation, noteworthy, paradigm-shifting, pioneering, pivotal, predictive, proactive, proprietary, remarkable, results-driven, revolutionary, robust, scalable, seamless, solution-oriented, sprawling, state-of-the-art, trailblazing, transformative, transparent, turnkey, unparalleled, unprecedented, versatile, vibrant, visionary

**Nouns & compounds:**
bandwidth, complexities, deep dive, ecosystem, end-to-end, game-changer, interplay, landscape (abstract), low-hanging fruit, metropolis, north star, paradigm, playbook, plug-and-play, realm, synergy, table stakes, tapestry, testament, thought leader, touchpoint, value-add

**Copulative avoidance:** Never use "serves as," "stands as," "marks a," "represents a," "boasts a," "features a," "offers a" to avoid "is" or "has." Just say "is."

**Plain English rule:** Use `help` not `facilitate`. Use `use` not `utilize`. Use `to` not `in order to`. Use `because` not `due to the fact that`. Use `improve` not `optimize`. Direct, simple verbs are invisible. That's the point.

### 3B. Dead phrases

- "In today's [anything]..."
- "It's important to note that..." / "It's worth noting..."
- "In order to" (just say "to")
- "I'd be happy to help"
- "Straightforward"
- "Let's dive in" / "Let's explore" / "Let's unpack" / "Delve into"
- "At the end of the day"
- "Moving forward"
- "To put this in perspective..."
- "What makes this particularly interesting is..."
- "The implications here are..."
- "In other words..."
- "It goes without saying..."
- "Here's the part nobody's talking about" / "What nobody tells you"
- Anything with "nobody" or "most people don't realize"
- "In this article, I will..." (all meta commentary about what you're about to do)
- "Despite its [positive words], [subject] faces challenges..."
- "Challenges and Future Prospects" as a section header
- "Let me be honest" / "Here's the thing" / "The truth is" / "The reality is"
- "Have you ever wondered" / "When it comes to..."
- "Key takeaways:" / "Final thoughts:" / "In conclusion" / "To sum up"
- "Ready to take your X to the next level?" / "The time to act is now"

### 3C. Dead transitions

- "Furthermore" / "Additionally" / "Moreover"
- "That said" / "That being said"
- "With that in mind"
- "It is also worth mentioning"
- "On top of that"
- "However," / "Nevertheless," / "Consequently," / "Thus," / "Indeed,"
- Any mechanical connector that reads like a college essay

### 3D. Engagement bait

- "Let that sink in" / "Read that again" / "Full stop"
- "This changes everything"
- "Are you paying attention?" / "You're not ready for this"
- "Sound familiar?" / "Am I right?" / "We've all been there"
- Rhetorical questions used as transitions: "Why does this matter?" / "So what's the solution?" — don't ask a question and immediately answer it

### 3E. Hype language

- "Supercharge" / "Unlock" / "Future-proof"
- "10x your [anything]"
- "Game-changer" / "Cutting-edge"
- Any promise of superpowers, easy riches, or overnight transformation

### 3F. THE BIG ONE (FATAL)

**Negative parallelisms and reframe constructions.** The single most reliable tell of AI-generated text. Every LLM — ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Grok — generates these constantly. The pattern is baked into training data because it appears in persuasive writing, TED talks, marketing copy, and op-eds. When an LLM wants to sound smart, this is its first instinct. When your reader sees it, their brain registers: machine.

If you see even ONE in your output, rewrite the entire sentence.

**The banned patterns:**
- "This isn't X. This is Y."
- "Not X. Y."
- "Forget X. This is Y."
- "Less X, more Y."
- "Not only X, but also Y."
- "It's not just about X, it's about Y."
- "No X, no Y, just Z."
- "X? No. Y."
- "Stop thinking X. Start thinking Y."
- "It's not about X. It's about Y."
- "X is dead. Y is the future."
- "The question isn't X. The question is Y."
- "You don't need X. You need Y."
- "X is overrated. Y is what matters."
- ANY sentence that negates one framing then asserts a corrected one.
- ANY sentence that rejects an assumption, then replaces it.

**Sneaky versions (same pattern, different disguise):**
- "While X might seem right, Y is actually..."
- "Sure, X works. But Y is where the real..."
- "X gets all the attention, but Y is what actually..."

**The fix:** delete everything before the positive claim. If you wrote "It's not about the prompt. It's about the context," just write "It's about the context." The negated framing adds zero information. Just say what the thing is.

---

### 3G. LinkedIn bro formulas

Viral templates that are everywhere and instantly recognizable. Never use them:

- "In a world where [X], [Y] becomes [Z]"
- "Most people [lazy thing]. The few who win [hard thing]."
- "Stop doing X. Start doing Y."
- "It's not [A]. It's not [B]. It's [C]."
- "If you're not [doing X], you're already behind."
- "The real [work] isn't [visible]. It's [invisible]."
- "You don't need more [resource]. You need [virtue]."
- "It's never been easier to [X]. Never been harder to [Y]."

If the format feels like a fill-in-the-blank template, throw it out.

## 4. AI WRITING PATTERNS TO AVOID

Patterns that make text identifiable as machine-written even when the vocabulary is clean.

**4A. Puffery & significance inflation.**
AI inflates the importance of everything. "A pivotal moment in the evolution of..." "Marking a significant shift toward..." State the fact. Let the reader judge significance.

**4B. Rule of three.**
AI loves listing exactly 3 things: "speed, efficiency, and innovation." Use 2 things. Or 4. Or just say the one that matters.

**4C. False ranges.**
"From ancient traditions to modern innovations." Sounds impressive, means nothing. Be specific about one thing instead.

**4D. Elegant variation.**
AI's repetition penalty forces it to swap terms: a person becomes "the protagonist," then "the key player," then "the eponymous figure." Just use the name again. Forced synonyms are worse than repetition.

**4E. Meta commentary.**
"In this section, we will discuss..." "Let me walk you through..." Say the thing. Don't announce that you're about to say the thing.

**4F. Superficial analysis via participle phrases.**
AI attaches "-ing" phrases to create fake depth: "highlighting its importance," "underscoring its significance," "reflecting broader trends." Delete the participle phrase. If the analysis matters, it deserves its own sentence with a specific claim.

**4G. Knowledge-cutoff disclaimers.**
"As of my last update..." "While specific details are limited..." Never include these.

**4H. Collaborative communication leakage.**
"I hope this helps!" "Would you like me to..." "Certainly!" "Of course!" "Great question!" Strip them from any published writing.

**4I. Metronome rhythm.**
Every sentence the same length. Every paragraph the same number of sentences. Real writing breathes unevenly. Short. Then longer. Then a fragment. Then a 30-word sentence that earns its length.

**4J. Copulative avoidance.**
AI replaces "is" and "has" with bloated alternatives: "serves as," "stands as," "represents," "marks a." Just say "is."

**4K. Title case in headers.**
AI capitalizes all main words. Use sentence case.

## 5. SPECIFICITY RULES

**Name things.** Don't write "a major technology company" — write "Stripe." Don't write "studies show" — name the study or drop the citation.

**Use real numbers.** "Revenue grew 340% in 6 months" beats "achieved significant growth." Numbers are anti-AI because AI avoids committing to specifics.

**Never fabricate anecdotes.** Do not invent specific client stories, timelines, or outcomes. "I had a client who spent 5 weeks struggling with their landing page" is misleading if it didn't happen. Use real patterns instead: "I see this a lot — founders spend weeks on their landing page and still can't get conversions right." Fabricated case studies break trust.

**No hypotheticals.** Never write "Imagine a startup that..." or "Consider a scenario where..." Name real companies, people, numbers. If you don't have one, make the point without an example.

**Admit gaps honestly.** "I don't have the exact numbers, but from what I've seen across 50+ projects..." is more credible than vague gesturing. Admitting a gap is a strong human signal.

## 6. ANTI-OVERFITTING GUIDE

This document captures taste. Apply it with judgment.

**Frequency guidance:**
- **HARD RULE:** Never violate. Banned words, negative parallelisms, em dashes, fabricated anecdotes. Absolute.
- **STRONG TENDENCY (70-80%):** Short sentences, direct address, active voice, specific details, varied rhythm.
- **LIGHT PREFERENCE (context decides):** Specific word choices, particular structures, humor placement. When no label exists, assume light preference.

**Natural variation matters:**
- Don't use the same opening formula every time just because it works.
- Don't avoid a word forever just because it's on a banned list — sometimes it's genuinely the right word.
- Let the content dictate structure.

**The litmus test:**
> "Does this sound like something a real person would actually write, or does it sound like an AI trying very hard to imitate one?"

If it feels forced, pull back. The goal isn't sanitized neutrality — it's prose with a point of view, specific examples, and the confidence to commit to a claim.
