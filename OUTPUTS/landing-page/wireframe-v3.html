03 — The problem

Most landing pages look fine. They just don't convert.

After 50+ audits, the same 3 issues come up every time.

Problem 01

The copy explains the product instead of the outcome

You've got a great product and the page is full of feature lists. But visitors don't want to know how it works. They want to know what changes for them. Features as headlines means visitors leave.

Problem 02

There's no single clear next step

Three buttons compete on one screen. Or the CTA only appears once, at the bottom. Visitors don't know what to do so they don't do anything. That's a conversion problem, not a traffic problem.

Problem 03

The hierarchy is backwards

Visitors read the wrong things first, miss the trust signals, and hit the CTA before they're convinced. A page with good design and the wrong reading order still doesn't convert. The fix usually takes a day.

12 — FAQ

Questions founders usually ask.

How fast do you actually start?

Same day or next. After we agree on scope, I send a short questionnaire — what the product does, who it's for, what the goal of the page is, what you've tried before. First visuals follow within 72 hours of getting that back. No kickoff call required, though I'm happy to do one if you want to talk it through first.

What do you need from me to get started?

The questionnaire answers, any existing brand assets (logo, colors, fonts if they exist), a few examples of sites you like, and a clear goal for the page. That's it. The simpler and more direct the brief, the faster I move. Vague briefs mean slower work and more revision rounds.

Do you write the copy?

No. I don't do copywriting. I'll restructure what you give me — sharpen the headline hierarchy, fix the CTA, call out when something isn't landing — and I'll tell you exactly what's missing. But the words come from you or your copywriter. If you don't have copy yet, I can work from a brief and use placeholder copy to show the structure, but the final words are your job.

What does "CRO" mean in practice on a landing page?

It means the page is built to convert visitors, not just look good. Hierarchy is set so visitors read the right things in the right order. CTAs appear where people are most ready to act. Social proof shows up before objections do. The layout guides someone from "what is this?" to "I want this" without them having to think. Most pages have the right content in the wrong order. That's usually where I start.

Is $10K for landing page + Webflow the only option?

No. If you just need a Figma design to hand to your own developer, that's $5K. If you need a full multi-page website, that starts at $15K and gets scoped individually. And if you want ongoing design support — updates, new pages, CRO tests as you run traffic — the retainer is $5,999/month, month-to-month.

Do you work with companies outside the US?

Yes. Most of my clients are US-based (Austin, NYC, SF mostly), but I'm based in Prague and work async-first. The timezone shift works in your favour — you send feedback at end of your day and wake up to revisions. I take calls in the evenings to cover the US overlap.

Case Study humbldesign.io/work/statdoctor
Landing page HealthTech Webflow 5 days

5 days to a landing page that actually books consultations.

StatDoctor connects patients with real doctors for fast online consultations. Their landing page was live. The booking rate wasn't where it needed to be. Here's what was wrong and how we fixed it.

Client
StatDoctor
Service
Landing page redesign + Webflow
Timeline
5 days
Industry
HealthTech

The brief

Dr. Anu G came through an audit offer. StatDoctor's core proposition is simple and genuinely useful: get a real doctor consultation online, quickly, without the wait. The landing page had been built and launched, but the booking rate was disappointing.

She wanted a redesign. The brief was clear: make visitors understand what StatDoctor does and get them to book a consultation.

What was actually broken

The page had three problems working against each other.

First, the hero led with features. "Online consultations with qualified doctors, available 24/7." Technically accurate, tells you nothing about what changes for you as a patient. The outcome — fast access to a real doctor without sitting in a waiting room — was buried two sections down.

Second, there was no trust signal above the fold. Healthcare is a high-trust category. People want to see credentials, doctor photos, or some form of social proof before they're willing to book. The existing page asked for a booking before it earned one.

Third, the CTA appeared once, at the bottom. Most visitors never got there.

[Old page — hero section]
[Old page — CTA section]

The work

Rebuilt the page hierarchy from the top down.

New hero: led with the outcome. "See a doctor today. No waiting room." Below that: a single CTA (Book a consultation), plus two trust signals — number of consultations completed, and a doctor credibility marker. Nothing else above the fold.

Trust section moved up, directly after the hero. Doctor photos, credentials, and a patient quote. This is where visitors decide whether StatDoctor is legit. It needed to come before any feature explanation, not after it.

The second CTA appeared after the trust section, where visitors are most primed to act. One action on the whole page: book a consultation.

Visual system: clean, approachable, clinical without being cold. The existing brand worked fine — this was a hierarchy and structure problem, not a brand problem. No redesign for redesign's sake.

Landing page FinTech Webflow 4+ years

$12M raised. The client I've been working with longest.

Augment is a private markets platform that gives high-net-worth investors access to alternative investments. We started with a landing page in 2021. Four years later, we're still building.

Client
Augment
Service
Landing page + ongoing design
Timeline
4+ years (ongoing)
Industry
FinTech

The brief

Augment helps investors access private markets — private equity, real estate, hedge funds, alternative assets that typically require institutional access or very high minimums. The product is genuinely valuable and genuinely complex.

The existing site tried to explain all of it upfront. The result: investors who weren't already familiar with alternative investments bounced immediately. The founders needed a landing page that could build trust first and explain the product second.

What was actually broken

The page led with product mechanics. Detailed explanations of how the investment process works, what asset classes were available, the fee structure. All of it accurate. All of it too early.

Investors who aren't already in the alternative investment space need to understand why they should care before they care about how it works. The existing hierarchy assumed prior knowledge that most visitors didn't have.

Trust signals — team credentials, regulatory compliance markers, existing investor names — were buried at the bottom. On a platform asking people to think about investing money, those signals needed to be visible within the first scroll.

The work

Rebuilt the landing page around a different reading order: investor outcome first, credibility second, product explanation third.

Hero: what do you get as an investor, in plain language. Not "access to alternative investments" (jargon) but the specific outcome that matters — diversification beyond stocks and bonds, access to asset classes that outperform public markets over 10-year periods.

Trust section: team backgrounds, regulatory status, names of existing investors. This came before any product explanation, because it answered the first question any serious investor asks.

Product section: now that the visitor trusts Augment, explain how it actually works. Same content as before, different position on the page.

Built in Webflow, launched, and kept going. New pages added as the product grew. CRO tweaks as conversion data came in. What started as a project became a standing arrangement — I'm faster than hiring someone new each time, and I already know the product and the founders' thinking.

Emmanuel Gatwech had an AI supply chain concept and a 24-hour window to get something in front of investors. No existing UX. No visual language. Just a clear idea and a tight deadline.

Client
Sahil

The brief

Sahil is an AI-powered platform that helps small businesses manage procurement and supplier relationships. The product simplifies supply chain complexity — comparing suppliers, ranking products, managing orders — through automation and a clean interface.

Emmanuel came with a concept and a problem: investors couldn't grasp the value from what he had. He needed product mockups that could show the product clearly, in 24 hours.

What we were working with

No UX structure. No user flows. The concept existed in Emmanuel's head and in rough notes. The core idea was strong — simplify procurement for small businesses using AI — but there was nothing visual to present to investors.

The challenge wasn't the deadline. Fast is doable with clear direction. The challenge was making a complex product (AI ranking algorithms, multiple user types, supplier and buyer flows) feel immediately understandable in a mockup.

The work

Short alignment call. One hour. Defined the core: what does a business owner actually do on Sahil? We stripped it to three flows: browse suppliers, compare and select products, place and track orders.

Opened Figma and started. Kept the UI minimal — the goal was to make the concept legible, not to build a final product. Clean typography, clear hierarchy in each screen, consistent navigation. Iterated with Emmanuel throughout the day, in real time over messages. He'd react to a screen, I'd adjust, move to the next.

By the end of 24 hours: a full set of screens covering the three core flows, at a quality level that read as a real product rather than a sketch.

Case Study humbldesign.io/work/castingofka
Platform redesign SaaS Web + mobile 3 months

3 months to unify a creative platform across web and mobile.

Castingofka connects actors, models, and extras with film and TV productions. The product worked. The experience felt like two different apps that happened to share a name.

Client
Castingofka
Service
Platform redesign (product design add-on)
Timeline
3 months
Industry
SaaS / Creative

The brief

Castingofka is a Prague-based platform where actors, models, and extras register, build portfolios, and connect with casting directors and production companies. A real product with real users — they knew it worked, and they wanted to know if it could feel better.

This project is product design, not landing page work. It's an example of the add-on service available to existing clients. The brief was a full platform redesign across web and mobile, focused on usability and consistency.

What was actually broken

Users bounced between a website and a mobile app that felt like different products. Different navigation logic. Different visual language. Different flows for the same tasks. People completing their profile on web would get confused when they picked up the mobile app, and vice versa.

The registration process was too long. Profile completion rates were lower than they should have been for a product people found genuinely useful. The platform had a retention problem that was really an experience problem.

[Old web platform — navigation and dashboard]
[Old mobile app — same screen, different design]

The work

Started where the data pointed. Mapped where users spent the most time and where they dropped off. The profile creation flow was the highest-impact place to start — low completion rate, high drop-off at step 3 of 7.

Redesigned the registration flow first: cut 7 steps to 4 by combining fields that didn't need to be separate screens and moving optional information out of the required flow. Completion rate improved immediately.

Navigation system next. Built one information architecture that worked across both web and mobile. Same labels, same order, same mental model. Users who switched between devices now had consistent expectations.

Visual layer last. One design system — component library, color tokens, typography scale — applied across both platforms. The internal team got the library too, so they could maintain consistency going forward without coming back to me for every update.
