## Brand Journey Framework (Caleb's 4 questions)

The four questions that anchor everything below.

**1. Outcome (Dec 31, 2026):** 30-50K LinkedIn followers, 15K newsletter subscribers, a designer audience that knows and trusts David. In 2027, founder retainer leads start flowing from that audience at scale.

**2. What David needs to be known for:** The conversion designer who teardowns landing pages on Loom. The person who teaches designers how to make pages convert. The 20K-downloads Figma template guy who turned into a creator.

**3. What David needs to do:** Publish weekly redesigns of real landing pages (the Reddit engine on LinkedIn), run a weekly newsletter, post 5 times a week across 5 pillars, run a tight piggyback comment system, build 4-6 freebies that become lead-magnet post fuel.

**4. What David needs to learn:** CRO research per industry (AI/SaaS/Fintech first), the design creator economy (who's posting what, what's working), conversion psychology applied to design work, newsletter growth mechanics.

## The contrarian belief (the brand spine)

**"Pretty doesn't convert. Conversion is the only design metric that matters."**

Every post defends this position. It pushes against the design community ("design is craft") and the marketing community ("CRO is our turf"). It justifies the entire content angle. It's debatable enough to drive engagement.

Sub-beliefs that live underneath:

- Designers undersell themselves because they don't track conversions.
- Good design is invisible. If visitors have to think, the page isn't converting.
- Vibe-coded landing pages are fine. The problem is everything that comes after them.
- Fix the landing page before running ads.

## The bet, in one paragraph

David's LinkedIn account becomes a designer-creator brand for 6 months. Designers engage, repost, and distribute. Founders skim and disappear. By writing for Alex (designer) first instead of Ryan (founder) first, the algorithm gets fed strong engagement signals, the audience builds fast, and Ryan-shaped founders eventually surface through three mechanisms: overflow referrals from Alex, indirect distribution to founders in Alex's network, and audience-size as a trust signal once the account clears 30K. Humbl Design (the studio) keeps its founder positioning on humbldesign.io. David Pokorny (the creator) becomes the designer-facing brand on LinkedIn. They run in parallel.

## Content pillars (5 main + 1 cross-cutting)

### Interest pillars (top of funnel, for Alex, 70% of rotation)

**1. Landing page redesigns (the engine).**
The Reddit pipeline brought to LinkedIn. 6 already recorded plus 10 in queue equals 16 redesigns ready. Each one produces a 4-piece content pack:
- LinkedIn carousel (Mon or Fri slot)
- Newsletter feature (expanded version, Loom embedded)
- Blog post on humbldesign.io (SEO-friendly)
- Short clip (60-second Loom highlight)

Source mix: solo founders from Reddit submissions, vibe-coded pages, famous public companies (Linear, Cursor, Vercel, Stripe Atlas, Cluely, Lovable), YC W25/S25 launches.

2 posts/week minimum.

**2. CRO for designers (the wedge).**
The undertapped niche. Conversion principles taught from a designer's perspective. Marketers already have plenty of this content. Designers don't. Sample topics: why your hero copy converts worse than your client's competitor's, the 5 social proof patterns that work above the fold, hierarchy mistakes designers make on pricing pages, what "conversion-focused" actually means in Figma, the CTA placement designers always get wrong.

1 post/week.

**3. Design industry takes.**
Opinions on tools, agencies, careers, freelancing in the AI era. Designjoy retainers vs project work, v0/Lovable/Bolt killing certain types of design jobs, the end of the Dribbble shot portfolio, why most design agencies sell the wrong thing.

Posted Thursday (when LinkedIn feed is most active for debate).

1 post/week.

**4. Design breakdowns.**
Small, granular, visual. The format David's past data already proved works. Buttons, hero sections, forms, nav patterns, empty states, loading states, mobile breakpoints. Single image or short carousel, 3-5 design observations, no philosophy.

1 post/week.

### Expert pillars (middle and bottom of funnel, for Ryan, 30% of rotation)

**5. Humbl Audit, Website Page CRO series.**
Every standard startup page, one by one, with the CRO blocks each needs. Founder-buyer content but Alex-readable framing. Series plan in `OUTPUTS/content-ideas/website-pages-cro-series.md`.

1 post/week, Wednesday.

**6. Case studies and proof.**
Real client stories. Augment $12M raise, StatDoctor 5-day landing page, the 20K Figma template downloads. Trust-building for founders.

Run every 2-3 weeks.

### Cross-cutting: Humbl Rules

Typographic posters used as filler across both interest and expert rotations. 16 rules already built using the generator at `OUTPUTS/linkedin-strategy/humbl-rule-generator.html`. Expansion to 50 rules planned as a freebie (see roadmap below).

## The Reddit redesign pipeline as the content engine

Current state: founder submits site on Reddit, David records Loom, sends back, cycle ends.

New pipeline: founder submits, David records Loom, asks permission to publish, publishes the 4-piece content pack.

**Permission template:**
"Hey, the review went well. Mind if I share the redesign as a teardown post? I'll credit you and link your site." Most founders say yes because it's free distribution for them.

**Inbound trigger on LinkedIn:**
"DM 'REVIEW' with your URL. Free 3-minute Loom review of your landing page. No catch. I publish them with permission only."

**The vibe-coded angle:**
Reddit submissions are mostly solo founders running vibe-coded pages. This is the wedge content. Long-form blog idea, then condensed LinkedIn carousel: "The 8 things I keep fixing on vibe-coded landing pages." Publish in month 1.

**Math:** 16 redesigns x 4 content pieces each = 64 pieces of content already half-built. At 5 posts/week with half from the redesign engine, that's 13 weeks of content sitting in inventory.

### Custom feed setup (the Sanders trick)

LinkedIn search bar, enter any term, results page, click "Posts" tab. In the left sidebar filter, pick "From" and add the creators above. Save that filter URL as a browser bookmark. Open once a day. That's the only feed worth scrolling.

Set notifications on the top 8 creators (the ones most likely to post outlier content). Comment within the first hour.

### Outlier hunting (weekly, 30 min)

Open the custom feed. Scan for posts that have clearly outperformed the creator's baseline (3x normal comments, disproportionate reposts, lots of tag-a-colleague). For each outlier, write down what format (carousel / image / text / lead-magnet / story), what hook structure, what made it shareable. Build a Humbl version using a different topic from the pillar list. Copy the format. The topic stays yours.

Sanders' rule: when something works, double down inside the same week. If a famous-site teardown carousel hits, run 3 more famous-site teardown carousels in the next 7 days, different sites. Don't spread out the wins.

## The 30/30/30/10 daily comment system

Five substantive comments per day, 15+ words each.

- **30% on large creators** (Pillars 1-4 list). Goal: borrow reach.
- **30% on industry voices** (Pillar 5 list: Peep, Andy, Talia). Goal: get known in the CRO community.
- **30% on the designer ICP audience** (peer-level designers, 1K-10K followers, posting about landing pages, conversion, redesigns). Goal: where future newsletter subs come from.
- **10% on warm network** (existing clients, friends, designers David already knows).

Comment voice rules in `content-rules.md` apply. Casual, fragments, lowercase fine. Never link to own content in comments. 15+ words minimum.

## The trust-action quality bar

Every post passes one question (per Caleb Ralston): "Does this make it easier for Alex to take action and get a result?"

Posts that pass also tend to be repostable (Sanders' #1 metric). The two bars stack:

1. Trust-action: would Alex be able to implement this on a client project Monday?
2. Repostable: would Alex share this in their designer Slack to look smart?

Posts that hit both are the strongest. Posts that hit only one still work. Posts that hit neither get scrapped.

## Metrics, layered

The current plan put everything on AUDIT DMs. For the 6-month audience build, that's wrong. Use a 3-tier setup.

### Tier 1, audience (the new headline metric)

- Newsletter subs per week (target: 200/week by month 3)
- Newsletter total (target: 15K by Dec 31)
- LinkedIn follower growth per week (target: 1,000+/week by month 3)
- LinkedIn total (target: 30-50K by Dec 31)

### Tier 2, distribution (leading indicators for tier 1)

- Reposts per post (target: 5%+ of impressions, Sanders' #1 signal)
- Comments per post (target: 50+/post by month 2)
- Comments from founder-shaped accounts on designer-aimed posts (proves the bridge works)
- Profile views per week
- Newsletter open rate (target: 40%+) and click rate (target: 5%+)

### Tier 3, latent buyer pipeline (passive, tracked but not pushed)

- AUDIT DMs per week (track, don't push)
- REVIEW DMs per week (the new free-redesign trigger)
- Inbound retainer inquiries (track, don't push)

The flip is intentional for the 6-month build. Conversion was tier 1. Now it's tier 3. Audience is tier 1. The tiers reverse in 2027.

## Risks and mitigations

**Risk: Designer audience drowns out founder positioning.**
Mitigation: humbldesign.io stays founder-aimed. Featured section still surfaces AUDIT. Founder buyers who matter look at the website and case studies. Both stay tuned for them.

**Risk: Reposts climb, AUDIT DMs stay flat.**
Mitigation: this is the diagnostic. Tier 2 is working, tier 1 isn't. Fix the bridge: more case studies, more "you're losing demos because of X" posts. The Website Page CRO series is the bridge, run it weekly without fail.

**Risk: Newsletter eats time that should go to LinkedIn posts.**
Mitigation: Newsletter writes itself from the redesign engine. Every issue is the LinkedIn carousel expanded plus one CRO principle. 90 minutes per issue once the format is set.

**Risk: Designer creator economy is too crowded.**
Mitigation: The CRO-for-designers wedge is the differentiator. Most design creators teach craft. Almost nobody teaches conversion outcomes. Stay in the wedge.

**Risk: 30-50K is unrealistic and we miss badly.**
Mitigation: 30K is the floor, 50K is stretch. Even 20K plus 10K newsletter subs is a strong outcome that gives 2027 plenty to work with.

**Risk: David hates pillar 3 (design industry takes) because it feels like guru content.**
Mitigation: only post pillar 3 takes that come from a real client situation or a real product David uses. No abstract takes. If there's no real story, skip the post.
