# LinkedIn growth strategy v2: designer-creator pivot

**Logged:** May 17, 2026
**Status:** Live plan. Replaces v1 (`designer-first-pivot-strategy.md`) and overrides the founder-only top-of-funnel in `humbl-linkedin-action-plan.md`.
**Trigger:** Josh Sanders interview (Chris Donnelly's head of content) + David's own analysis that designer-aimed posts always outperformed founder-aimed ones.

## The bet, restated

David's LinkedIn becomes a designer-creator account for the next 6 months. Goal: 30-50K followers by Dec 31, 2026. Audience: designers who want to learn CRO, founders who happen to also follow, marketers in between. Distribution mechanic: weekly newsletter ("CRO for designers") + the Reddit redesign engine brought onto LinkedIn.

This is not abandoning the Humbl Design business. Humbl Design (the studio) keeps its current founder-aimed positioning on humbldesign.io. David Pokorny (the creator) becomes the designer-facing brand on LinkedIn. The two run in parallel for 6 months. In 2027, the audience size makes David credible to founders again, and the LinkedIn account re-converges back toward the founder funnel from a position of reach.

The pivot is rational because:

- Past data proves it. David's design-aimed posts outperformed every founder-aimed post. The audience already self-selected.
- Money pressure is off. No retainer needed in the next 6 months. Aim for audience growth. AUDIT DMs become a tracked-but-not-pushed metric.
- Founder buyers check follower count. Selling from 2K followers is harder than selling from 30K, even with worse content.
- The Reddit free redesign pipeline (6 recorded, 10 in queue) is a content engine sitting unused on the wrong platform.
- "CRO for designers" is an undertapped niche. Most design creators teach UI craft. Almost nobody teaches conversion outcomes.

## New content pillars (5 pillars, 5 posts/week)

### Pillar 1: Landing page redesigns (the Reddit engine)

The biggest pillar. This is the format you already have working. Bring it from Reddit to LinkedIn.

**Format options per redesign:**
- Carousel: "I redesigned [X]'s landing page. Here's what was broken." 8-12 slides. Before screenshot, problem callouts, redesigned version, summary of changes.
- Loom embed: 3-minute Loom of the review. People share Loom links because they make the sharer look smart.
- Side-by-side image: one shot, before/after, with 3 fix bullets in the caption.

**Source mix:**
- Solo founders / vibe-coded pages from Reddit submissions (the existing pipeline)
- Famous public companies (Linear, Cursor, Vercel, Stripe Atlas), the ones that get reposted most
- YC W25 / S25 launches (timely, designers and founders both follow YC)

**Cadence:** 2 redesign posts per week minimum. This is the engine.

### Pillar 2: CRO for designers (educational)

Conversion principles taught for designers. Marketers already get this content elsewhere. Designers don't. The angle nobody else owns.

Topics:
- "Why your hero copy converts worse than your client's competitor"
- "The 5 social proof patterns that actually work above the fold"
- "Hierarchy mistakes designers make on pricing pages"
- "What 'conversion-focused' actually means in Figma"
- "How to read a heatmap (without losing 2 hours)"
- "The CTA placement designers always get wrong"

This is the niche wedge. Designers know UI. Most don't know conversion psychology. You teach the gap and they remember you for it.

**Cadence:** 1 post per week.

### Pillar 3: Design industry takes

Opinions on tools, AI, agencies, careers, freelancing. Drives engagement (designers argue in comments).

Topics:
- v0 / Lovable / Bolt: which jobs they're killing and which they're not
- Designjoy retainer model: still good in 2026 or dead?
- The "designer who can code" arc, two years in
- Why design school is wrong about portfolios
- AI tools that actually save designer hours
- Junior designer career paths in a vibe-coded world

These get reposted by designers because the takes are debatable and designers love a take.

**Cadence:** 1 post per week (Thursday, when the feed is most active).

### Pillar 4: Design breakdowns

Small, granular, visual. The posts your data already proved work.

Topics:
- Buttons (the post that worked before)
- Hero sections, side-by-side
- Form fields and microcopy
- Nav patterns
- Empty states
- Loading states
- Mobile breakpoints on landing pages

Format: single image or short carousel, 3-5 design observations, no philosophy.

**Cadence:** 1 post per week.

### Pillar 5: Humbl Rules

Carries over from the existing strategy. Punchy heuristics, typographic posters, one principle per image. 16 already built using the generator in `OUTPUTS/linkedin-strategy/humbl-rule-generator.html`.

**Cadence:** Slotted in as needed (1 per week or as filler).

## The Reddit redesign pipeline becomes the content engine

Right now: founder submits site on Reddit → David records Loom → sends back → cycle ends.

New pipeline: founder submits site on Reddit → David records Loom → asks for permission to publish → publishes 4-piece content pack from each redesign.

### The 4-piece content pack per redesign

1. **LinkedIn carousel** (Mon or Fri slot). 8-12 slides: brief intro to the site, 3 things wrong, 3 fixes, before/after image.
2. **Newsletter feature.** Same redesign, expanded. 5-7 minutes read. Why each fix matters from a CRO angle. Include the Loom embedded.
3. **Blog post on humbldesign.io.** SEO-friendly version. Title pattern: "Landing page review: [Company name]'s [specific problem]". Drives long-tail search.
4. **Short-form clip.** 60-second Loom highlight (the punchiest moment). Posted as native LinkedIn video or used as a teaser in the carousel.

16 Reddit redesigns = 64 pieces of content. That's the next 13 weeks of LinkedIn content if you run 5 posts/week and half of them come from the redesign engine.

### Submission flow

The Reddit thread already works. Move the inbound DM trigger onto LinkedIn too:

"DM 'REVIEW' with your URL. Free 3-minute Loom review of your landing page. No catch. I publish them with permission only."

Permission template: "Hey, the review went well. Mind if I share the redesign as a teardown post? I'll credit you and link your site." Most founders say yes because it's free distribution.

### The vibe-coded angle

The Reddit submissions are mostly solo founders running vibe-coded pages. That's a wedge. Designer audience finds vibe-code-vs-designer takes fun. Founder audience finds them useful. Specific blog post idea David mentioned: "The 8 things I keep fixing on vibe-coded landing pages." Run this as a long-form blog + condensed LinkedIn carousel + newsletter feature within month 1.

## Risks and how to handle them

**Risk: The newsletter eats time that should go into LinkedIn posts.**
Mitigation: Newsletter writes itself from the redesign engine. Every newsletter is essentially the LinkedIn carousel expanded plus one CRO principle. 90 minutes per issue once the format is set.

**Risk: Founder buyers see designer content and skip Humbl entirely.**
Mitigation: humbldesign.io stays founder-aimed. Featured section on LinkedIn still surfaces the audit. The DM 'AUDIT' trigger still works. Founder buyers who matter will look at the website and the case studies. Both are still tuned for them.

**Risk: Designer audience is too crowded to break through.**
Mitigation: The CRO-for-designers wedge is the differentiator. Most design creators teach craft (Figma tips, UI moves). Almost nobody teaches conversion outcomes. Stay in the wedge, don't drift to "design tips" generic content.

**Risk: Reddit submitters stop coming.**
Mitigation: Once LinkedIn flow is live, REVIEW DMs replace Reddit submissions as the inbound. Reddit becomes secondary, LinkedIn primary.

**Risk: 30-50K is unrealistic and we miss badly.**
Mitigation: 30K is the floor target, 50K is the stretch. Even 20K with a 10K newsletter is a strong outcome for 6 months and gives 2027 plenty to work with.
