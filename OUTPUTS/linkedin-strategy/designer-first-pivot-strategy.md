# Designer-first pivot: LinkedIn growth strategy

**Logged:** May 17, 2026
**Status:** New plan, replaces the founder-only top-of-funnel approach in `humbl-linkedin-action-plan.md`
**Trigger:** Josh Sanders interview (Chris Donnelly's head of content). Two ideas David wants applied: interest vs expert pillars, and the piggyback creator system (~min 40).

---

## The bet, in one paragraph

Humbl's account has been built to talk straight to Ryan. Ryan is the right buyer. Ryan is also a terrible LinkedIn engager. Founders skim, save, and move on. Designers stop, comment, repost, and tag colleagues. If the first 10K followers are mostly designers, the algorithm gets fed strong engagement signals on every post, which then pushes the same posts into the wider feed where founders see them. Designers won't DM "AUDIT." They become the distribution engine that puts Humbl in front of the founders who will.

This is exactly Sanders' interest pillar logic. Broad pillars get you the reach. Expert pillars convert that reach. Run both, weighted toward interest at the top.

- Master positioning line stays. "I design high-converting landing pages and websites for early-stage AI, SaaS, and Fintech founders. DM 'AUDIT' with your URL."
- ICP stays Ryan. Designers are the audience that helps Ryan find Humbl. They aren't the buyer.

- The pinned Humbl Audit post stays the always-on conversion asset.
- The DM flow stays. The audit offer stays.
- The North Star metric stays AUDIT DMs per week.

What changes is the content mix and the engagement system feeding it. The bottom of the funnel is fine. The top is thin.

## New pillar structure: 4 interest, 2 expert

Sanders runs 5-7 pillars on a high-growth account. Three is too few for a daily cadence and limits reach. Here's the re-cut.

### Interest pillars (top of funnel, for designers, marketers, and the broader startup feed)

**1. Landing page teardowns of famous sites.**
Pick public companies and recent YC launches. Rewrite their hero, fix their pricing page, mark up their above-the-fold. Screenshot plus red callouts plus the rewrite. Designers love this format. Founders save it. Marketers repost it. Same skill that Humbl sells, just aimed at sites the audience already knows.

Examples to teardown over 30 days: Linear, Vercel, Notion, Figma, Cluely, Cursor, Lovable, v0, Stripe Atlas, Pylon, Granola, Browser Company, Clay.

**2. The design industry, with opinions.**
Designjoy retainers vs project work. v0 and Lovable killing certain types of design jobs. The end of the "Dribbble shot" portfolio. Why most design agencies sell the wrong thing. Designers click on every one of these. Founders pick up that David has range and a point of view.

**3. AI in design and on landing pages.**
Tools designers are using. Tools clients are using. What AI changes for CRO. What it doesn't. Concrete: prompts that work, side-by-side outputs, screenshots. This pillar travels far because it's the most-searched topic on LinkedIn right now.

**4. Humbl Rules. Punchy heuristics.**
Already a pillar. Keeping it but moving it into "interest" because it's the most shareable format on the account. Each rule is one screenshot, one statement, one principle from 50+ audits. Designers post-share these. Founders screenshot them. Same content, more often, with the new image generator already built.

### Expert pillars (middle and bottom of funnel, for Ryan)

**5. Humbl Audit: Website Page CRO series.**
The existing series in `OUTPUTS/content-ideas/website-pages-cro-series.md`. Every standard startup page, one by one, with the CRO blocks each needs. This is the founder-buyer content. Ryan reads it and recognizes 4 of the 8 mistakes on his own site.

**6. Case studies and proof.**
Real client stories. The Augment $12M raise. StatDoctor 5-day landing page. The 20K Figma template downloads. These build trust and feed the DM flow. Run weekly. Daily would burn the format and pull from the higher-reach pillars.

### Daily rotation (5 posts/week)

| Day | Pillar | Type | Audience pulled |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mon | 1, Famous site teardown | Carousel + before/after | Designers + marketers + founders |
| Tue | 4, Humbl Rule | Typographic poster | All |
| Wed | 5, Website Page CRO | Carousel | Founders (Ryan) |
| Thu | 2 or 3, Industry opinion or AI take | Image + take | Designers + marketers |
| Fri | 6, Case study or 4, Humbl Rule | Story post | Founders + designers |

The 70/30 weight (interest/expert) is intentional. Sanders explicitly says: heavy on interest pillars if growth is the goal, heavy on expert pillars if direct lead-gen short-term is the goal. Humbl needs growth right now to make the lead-gen content reach anyone at all.

### The custom feed setup (the Sanders trick)

LinkedIn search bar, enter any term, results page, click "Posts" tab. In the left sidebar filter, pick "From" and add multiple creators from above. Save that filter URL as a browser bookmark. Open it once a day. That's the only feed worth scrolling.

Set notifications on the top 8 creators (the ones most likely to post outlier content). Comment within the first hour.

### The 30/30/30/10 daily comment system

Sanders' rule. Five substantive comments a day, allocated:

- **30% (about 1-2 comments/day) on large creators.** Pillars 1-4 list. Goal: borrow reach. Comment must be sharp enough that their audience clicks David's profile.
- **30% (about 1-2 comments/day) on industry voices.** Pillar 5 list (Peep, Andy, Talia). Goal: get known inside the CRO community.
- **30% (about 1-2 comments/day) on Ryan-shaped accounts.** Pillar 6 list. Goal: research and direct visibility. If the founder doesn't post, comment on a colleague's post or the company page.
- **10% (about 3-4 comments/week) on warm-network and existing client posts.** Friends, past clients, designer peers.

Comment voice rules already in `content-rules.md` apply. Casual, fragments, lowercase fine. Never link to own content in comments. 15+ words minimum to count as substantive.

### Outlier hunting (do this weekly, 30 min)

Open the custom feed. Scan for posts that have clearly outperformed the creator's baseline. Look for:
- Post got 3x the comments of their normal range
- Post got disproportionate reposts (Sanders' #1 metric. Reposts mean the audience wanted to look smart sharing it)
- Comment section has people tagging colleagues

For each outlier, write down: what format (carousel / image / text / lead-magnet / story), what hook structure, what made it shareable. Then build a Humbl version using a different topic from the pillar list. The job is to copy the *format*. The topic is yours.

Sanders' specific tactic: when something works, double down inside the same week. If a famous-site teardown carousel hits, run 3 more famous-site teardown carousels in the next 7 days, different sites. Do not "spread out the wins."

## The repost is the #1 metric

Sanders is explicit: reposts beat likes beat comments beat views.

A repost means a designer or marketer made David's content look like *their* taste. That's distribution Humbl can't pay for. Every post should pass the "would a designer share this in a Slack #design channel?" test.

What makes a post shareable:
- Beautifully designed image (already a Humbl strength)
- Generic-enough framing that the sharer isn't outing their own client
- A clean principle, framework, or before/after. Personal anecdotes don't travel.
- No selfie. No "celebrating my X anniversary" energy. Those don't get reposted.

Personal posts (anniversaries, behind-the-scenes, vulnerable founder stuff) are fine for trust. They belong in pillar 6, run sparingly. They build warmth with people who already follow. They don't grow the account.

Tracking: open LinkedIn analytics after each post. Save the repost count at the 24h mark and 72h mark to a Notion sheet. Posts with >5% repost rate get re-run as a sibling post within 7 days, different topic, same format.

## Metrics, layered

The current plan put everything on AUDIT DMs. That's right for the bottom of the funnel but useless for tracking whether top-of-funnel growth is working. Use a 3-tier setup.

**Tier 1: Conversion (the only one that matters for revenue)**
- AUDIT DMs per week from founder accounts
- AUDIT DMs that turn into delivered audits
- Delivered audits that turn into retainer or one-off projects

**Tier 2: Distribution (leading indicators for tier 1)**
- Reposts per post (target: 5%+ of impressions)
- Comments from founder-shaped accounts on designer-aimed posts (proves the bridge is working)
- Ryan-shaped profile views per week (LinkedIn Premium required)

**Tier 3: Reach (vanity but useful early)**
- Follower growth, only until 10K
- Total impressions per week
- The custom-feed list growing (more creators worth tracking means the niche is alive)

The bet works if tier 3 grows fast for 60 days, tier 2 starts climbing by day 90, and tier 1 starts ticking by day 120. If tier 1 doesn't move by day 150, the bridge between designer content and founder DMs is broken and the rotation needs to shift back to founder-heavy.

## Risks and how to handle them

**Risk: Designer audience drowns out founder positioning.**
Mitigation: every post, regardless of pillar, ends with the same DM CTA. The bio, headline, banner, and pinned post stay 100% founder-aimed. Designers read the content and see the offer is for founders. Some will refer founders. Most won't, but they're paying for distribution with reposts.

**Risk: Reposts go up, AUDIT DMs stay flat.**
Mitigation: this is the diagnostic. It means tier 2 is working and tier 1 isn't. Fix the bridge: more case studies, more "you're losing demos because of X" posts that name founder pain in founder vocabulary. The CRO Page series is the bridge. Run it weekly without fail.

**Risk: Trying to track 60 creators is too much.**
Mitigation: Start with 24 (4 per pillar). Add only when one is removed. Sanders specifically warned that the bank should be tight.

**Risk: David hates the design-industry-opinion pillar because it feels like guru content.**
Mitigation: only post pillar 2 takes that come from a real client situation or a real product David uses. No abstract takes. If there's no real story, skip the post for that day.
