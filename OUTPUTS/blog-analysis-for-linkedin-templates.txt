[3] RECURRING THEMES, OPINIONS & STRONG STANCES
================================================================================

CORE THESIS: FRICTION > FEATURES
David's foundational argument across 15+ posts is that SaaS founders misdiagnose problems.
They blame pricing, features, or marketing. The real killer is invisible UX friction.
Applies to: onboarding, dashboards, login flows, headlines, hero sections, form fields.

CLARITY > BEAUTY (The Anti-Decoration Stance)
A visually plain, perfectly clear interface beats a beautiful but confusing one.
Decoration adds mental work. Clarity removes it. Design's job is removing obstacles, not adding polish.
Counterpoint to modern design trends that prioritize aesthetics over utility.

REAL PROOF > MARKETING COPY
Stop hiding your best assets (real screenshots, workflows, before/afters) behind Loom links.
Customer proof design puts messy, real moments next to CTAs so proof actually sells.
Vague headlines and polished mockups underperform actual user workflows and results.

DESIGN DEBT IS NOT OPTIONAL
Each "we'll fix it later" is a tax on users. Cluttered sidebars, extra fields, confusing flows.
Design debt directly impacts retention and MRR—it's not a tech debt problem engineers can ignore.
Small friction compounds. Eventually, users stop paying it and churn.

SPEED > PERFECTION
Fast-moving startups need senior design help without hiring/recruiting/onboarding overhead.
Retainers beat hourly models. Fixed-scope beats hourly. Both beat the hesitation of hourly billing.
1 hour focused design work beats 1 week of perfectionism.

PSYCHOLOGY DRIVES BEHAVIOR
Design decisions are psychology decisions: expectation setting, visual hierarchy, clarity of next step.
Login screens set the tone for product maturity. Headlines trigger emotional recognition ("this is for me").
Accessibility isn't a compliance box—it's about operational design across complex user states.

DESIGNERS MOVE UP OR GET SQUEEZED
AI will commoditize pixel production. The designers who survive own strategy, feedback loops, taste.
Judgment calls beat execution. Knowing *why* a design works beats knowing *how* to execute it.
This is the gap between senior strategy and junior pixel-pushing.

ACTIONABLE > THEORETICAL
Posts are frameworks, guides, checklists—not design philosophy essays or industry takes.
Every post answers: What's broken? How do I recognize it? Exactly how do I fix it?
This positions David as builder/consultant, not theorist.

SAAS IS THE PRIMARY LENS
Every post assumes SaaS context: growth metrics, conversions, MRR, pricing, retention, churn.
Applies to founders and product teams that ship fast and measure impact.
Not about branding, print design, or "design for design's sake."

ANTI-BULLSHIT STANCE
Titles use words like "killing," "wasting," "secretly," "mistakes," "wrong."
Rejects both extremes: "AI will replace designers" AND "AI is just a tool, relax."
Real talk about what's actually happening on product teams.

AI REALISM
Not hype. Not doom. Practical breakdowns of what AI does well (early exploration, ideation),
where it fails (judgment, taste, user feedback loops), and what that means for daily work.
Designers need to know which tools work, when, and what to avoid—not inspirational takes.

TECHNICAL DEPTH ON DESIGN SYSTEMS
Posts on color systems, accessibility standards, Webflow architecture, Figma workflows.
Systems thinking: how do foundational choices scale across complex applications?
Not tutorials—technical references for engineers and designers shipping real products.
