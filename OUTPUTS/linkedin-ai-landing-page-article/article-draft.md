# The HUMBL framework: how to use AI to architect a SaaS landing page that converts

Most SaaS landing pages sit at 1-2% conversion. Founders spend weeks on them. They obsess over Figma. They pick fonts.

Then paste in "Revolutionize your workflow" and wonder why nobody's clicking.

The order is wrong. The page was designed before anyone answered the conversion questions. Copy came last. Nobody asked what objections the visitor walks in with, what makes them trust you in 5 seconds, or who the page is actually for.

Here's the system I use to fix that across 50+ SaaS audits: one mega-prompt, one framework, a specific psychological sequence.

I call it HUMBL.

---

## Why the framework exists

Every page I've audited stuck at 1-2% has the same problems. Design before conversion thinking. Copy as an afterthought. No system, so every page starts from zero.

The HUMBL framework solves all three. It's the structure you feed into a mega-prompt before you open Figma. AI architects the blueprint. You build from the spec. Then you spend 2-3 hours on the human pass that turns a draft into something that converts.

That last part matters. AI gives you roughly 85% of a good landing page. The 15% you add yourself: the hero copy in the founder's actual voice, real testimonials with real numbers, CTA copy that's been tested. That's where the lift lives.

## The HUMBL framework

**H: Hook**
**U: Urgency**
**M: Mechanism**
**B: Believability**
**L: Lower barriers**

Five sections. This order. Every time.

[ILLUSTRATION: Vertical stack of 5 labeled section blocks. Each block: the letter, the section name, and one line describing its single job. Stripped-down, no design mockups.]

---

### H: Hook

**What you give AI:** your product in one sentence plus the single biggest pain your visitor walks in with.

**What AI outputs:** 3 headline variants, each under 8 words, each attacking that pain directly. Plus a subheadline showing the transformation after using the product.

**What you do:** pick the strongest variant and rewrite it in the founder's actual voice. Then add one primary CTA, one secondary for people not ready ("See how it works"), and a trust bar: logos, a stat, or a short testimonial.

No AI headline ships unedited. The hero is too important for that. But having 3 variants to choose from and edit is far faster than starting from a blank page.

One job: pass the 5-second test. If a visitor can't answer "what is this, why should I care, what do I do next" in 5 seconds, the page is over.

---

### U: Urgency

**What you give AI:** your ICP (specific: role, stage, revenue band, core pain) plus 3 real objections or fears that visitor has before they trust you.

**What AI outputs:** 3-4 pain bullets written in "you" language with concrete scenarios and numbers.

**What you do:** check every bullet against real user research or sales call notes. Replace anything AI imagined with something you've actually heard.

The difference between AI output and human-verified output here is significant. AI writes: "many businesses struggle with low conversion rates." You correct it to: "If you're spending $10K a month on ads and your page converts at 1%..." Specific always beats abstract.

---

### M: Mechanism

**What you give AI:** a list of your product's features plus the outcome each one produces for the user.

**What AI outputs:** feature descriptions rewritten as transformations, plus a 3-step "how it works" sequence (verb plus outcome per step).

**What you do:** verify every outcome claim is accurate, compress the steps to 3 if AI gave you more, and confirm the language matches how your actual users talk about results.

The prompt instruction that changes everything: "Describe each feature as the outcome the user gets, not the technical function." "AI-powered lead scoring" becomes "Spend 80% less time on leads that go nowhere." The visitor cares about where they're going, not how the engine works.

---

### B: Believability

**What you give AI:** your real proof. Actual client quotes, actual metrics, actual company names and roles.

**What AI outputs:** formatted testimonial blocks, a social proof section structure, and suggested placement for logos and stats.

**What you do:** nothing except verify the formatting looks right. The data comes entirely from you. AI just structures the presentation.

This is the section where quality is fully determined by your inputs. Good testimonials have 4 things: a concrete metric, a real name, a real role, a real company. "Great product, highly recommend" converts no one. "Our demo bookings tripled in 30 days" with a name and title attached does.

### L: Lower barriers

**What you give AI:** 5 real objections you've heard on sales calls or seen in support tickets. The actual words people use.

**What AI outputs:** FAQ entries for each objection, each ending with a soft reason to act. Plus a final CTA block: urgency headline, before/after recap, primary and secondary CTA, risk reversal line.

**What you do:** verify the FAQ answers are accurate and actually address the objection (AI sometimes deflects instead of answering). Check that every answer ends with something that moves the visitor forward, not just closes the objection.

The questions must come from reality, not AI's imagination. "What if I don't see results?" is a real objection. AI invented FAQs like "How does your platform handle enterprise security at scale?" are not. The difference is your sales call notes.

## Why this order is non-negotiable

[ILLUSTRATION: Top-to-bottom journey line. 5 stages: Attention (H), Relevance (U), Hope (M), Trust (B), Action (L). The letter next to each stage. Clean, minimal.]

**Hook** stops them. If the headline doesn't land in 5 seconds, the page is over.

**Urgency** creates relevance. They feel the pain. They keep reading.

**Mechanism** gives them hope. There's a way out. The product is the bridge.

**Believability** builds trust. People like them got results.

**Lower barriers** converts. Every objection handled, then one clear action.

That sequence is the whole job. Leading with mechanism before urgency skips the emotional hook. Skipping believability breaks trust right before the conversion. Every SaaS page stuck at 1-2% is missing one of these stages or has them in the wrong order.

## How to use this with AI

Before you write a single prompt, gather five things:

1. One-sentence product value
2. Specific ICP: role, stage, revenue band, core pain (not "B2B companies")
3. Top 3 objections a visitor walks in with
4. Tangible proof: metrics, logos, real quotes
5. One conversion goal: demo, trial, or waitlist

Feed all five into one mega-prompt that asks the AI to output the full HUMBL blueprint: copy for each section, CTA variants, build specs (hex codes, font pairing, spacing, responsive guidelines).

That's the Architect phase. Then you build. Then you spend 2-3 hours on the 15%: hero copy in your voice, real proof swapped in, CTA tested, mobile checked.

The single biggest shift: run the brief before opening Figma. That change alone moves pages.

## FAQs

**What AI model should I use for the mega-prompt?**

Any frontier model works: Claude, GPT-4, Gemini. The limiting factor is the brief, not the model. A vague brief gives generic output regardless of what you use. Invest in the inputs.

**How long does the Architect phase take?**

30-60 minutes to build the brief, 5-10 minutes to run the prompt, 30-45 minutes to review the output. Under 2 hours for a full HUMBL blueprint.

**Can a non-designer use this system?**

Yes, with one caveat. Architect and the optimization pass are accessible to anyone. Translating the blueprint into a layout needs some design judgment or a solid Webflow/Framer template. The system tells you what to build. Executing the layout is a separate skill.

**What's the most common mistake when using AI for landing pages?**

Shipping the first output. AI gives you a draft. The 15% pass is where most of the conversion lift lives. Founders who skip it then say AI-generated pages look generic. They do, if you ship them unedited.

**How many sections does a SaaS landing page actually need?**

HUMBL covers everything needed to convert a cold visitor. For an early MVP, the minimum is Hook, Believability, Mechanism, and a CTA. Don't add sections to add sections. Every section that doesn't move the visitor closer to converting gives them a reason to leave.
