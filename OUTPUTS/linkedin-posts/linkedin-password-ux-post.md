# LinkedIn Post — Password UX Tips

---

🔐 Designing better password UX. A few things worth rethinking ↓

Show requirements before the user fails, not after. Most forms wait until submission to reveal what went wrong. Show the rules inline from the first keystroke.

Add a strength meter. It shifts motivation from "comply with rules" to "make it stronger." Small change, measurable impact on password quality.

Kill the confirm password field. One input plus a show/hide toggle produces fewer errors than two blind fields.

Never block paste. Password managers depend on it.

Length beats complexity. "fish merchant tokyo glasses" is harder to crack than "Tr0ub4dor&3" and easier to remember. Passphrases over special character gymnastics.

Keep users logged in for 30+ days from the same browser and location. Require re-auth only for critical actions.

Use magic links for recovery. Security questions are guessable — most of the answers are on social media anyway.

Consider passkeys. 93% login success rate vs 63% for traditional auth. Login time drops from 31 seconds to 8. Use identifier-first flow and label it "face, fingerprint, or PIN" instead of "passkey."

Good auth happens when users don't have to remember, recover, or retype. That's the design goal.
