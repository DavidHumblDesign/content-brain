**Designers vs developers, the AI gap in 2026**

## Black tag block (under title, white text on black)

1,000+ tech workers were asked how AI shows up in their core work, how satisfied they are with the tools, and whether it improves their output. Designers and developers gave very different answers.

## Circle 1 — "Tool adoption" (top circle, dark navy)

**Label inside circle:**
**Tool adoption**
*How often AI shows up in someone's core day-to-day work.*

**Fan segments around circle 1:**

- **59%** — of **Developers** use AI for their core work — *HIGH* (big dark blue fan)
- **31%** — of **Designers** use AI for their core work — *LOW* (smaller fan)
- **82%** — of **Developers** are satisfied with their AI tools — *HIGH* (medium fan)
- **69%** — of **Designers** are satisfied with their AI tools — *HIGH* (cream/light fan)

## Circle 2 — "Quality lift" (bottom circle, dark navy)

**Label inside circle:**
**Quality lift**
*Whether AI is actually making the work better, not just faster.*

**Fan segments around circle 2:**

- **68%** — of **Developers** say AI improves their work quality — *HIGH* (big dark blue fan)
- **54%** — of **Designers** say AI improves their work quality — *LOW* (cream fan)
- **60%** — how far AI gets you on a real project before a human has to take over — *MIDDLE* (medium fan, this is the "60% problem")
- **40%** — the part where design actually lives, brand fit, user behavior, founder taste — *LOW* (small fan, paired with the 60%)

## Pull quote (black box, white text, "FINDINGS SUGGEST" style)

**FINDINGS SUGGEST:**
Code has to compile and pass tests, a narrow definition of correct. A design has to understand why users bail at step two of checkout. AI doesn't clear that bar yet.
