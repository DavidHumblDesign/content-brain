# SEO Metadata for Blog Posts

Every blog post must include an SEO metadata block delivered alongside the content. This powers discoverability in Google, AI search engines (Perplexity, ChatGPT, Google AI Mode), and Humbl Design's CMS.

---

## Required deliverables for every blog post

```
TITLE OPTIONS: [5 title options to choose from]
POST SUMMARY: [2-3 sentence summary of the post's argument and value]
SEO TITLE: [max 60 characters]
SEO DESCRIPTION: [max 155 characters]
SLUG: [url-friendly-slug]
TARGET KEYWORDS: [primary keyword, secondary keyword, long-tail keyword]
BLOG LISTING BLURB: [1-2 sentence description visible on the blog index page. Personal, specific, makes someone want to click.]
RELATED POSTS: [2-3 suggested related topics or existing post titles]
FAQS: [3-5 questions and answers based on the article content]
```

---

## Title options (5)

Generate 5 distinct title options for the post. These are the H1 / article title, not the SEO title (that's separate).

- Each title should take a slightly different angle or framing of the same topic.
- Mix styles: some direct and descriptive, some opinionated, some with a specific claim.
- All anti-AI rules apply — no banned words, no clickbait, no "You Won't Believe" bait.
- Be specific. "Why Most Startups Should Use a Design Retainer Before They Hire" is specific. "The Benefits of Design Retainers" is generic.
- David's voice should come through even in the title. Direct, opinionated, plain language.

---

## Post summary (2-3 sentences)

A concise summary of the post's core argument and what the reader gets from it. Used for social sharing previews, CMS cards, and internal reference. Keep it in David's voice — direct, specific, no filler. State the argument, not just the topic. "This post is about design retainers" is weak. "Most startups hire a designer before they're ready. A retainer gives you senior design help without the salary or onboarding" is strong.

---

## SEO title rules (max 60 chars)

The SEO title is what appears in Google search results. It's different from the article's H1.

- Answer-first: frame it as the answer to what a founder would actually search. "Why SaaS Landing Page Templates Kill Conversions" not "A Comprehensive Guide to Landing Page Strategy."
- Include the primary keyword naturally, ideally near the front.
- Be specific. Numbers work when natural, not forced. "5 UX Mistakes That Tank SaaS Signups" is specific. "UX Mistakes to Avoid" is generic.
- No clickbait. No "You Won't Believe." No question-baiting.
- No banned words in the SEO title either. "How to Elevate Your Design Process" is out.
- Keep David's voice even here. The SEO title should sound like something a smart person would click, not something a keyword tool spit out.

---

## SEO description rules (max 155 chars)

This is the snippet below the title in search results. It should make someone click.

- Summarize the specific value of the article in one or two sentences.
- Include the primary keyword naturally.
- Don't start with "In this article, we..." or "Learn how to..." — those are generic. Start with the insight or the payoff.
- Make it specific enough that a founder reads it and thinks "this is for me."

---

## Slug rules

- Lowercase, hyphen-separated, no stop words unless they aid readability.
- Keep it short and keyword-rich: `saas-landing-page-template-mistakes` not `why-you-shouldnt-use-templates-for-your-saas-landing-pages-a-guide`
- Match the primary keyword phrase closely.

---

## Target keywords

- Provide a primary keyword (the main search term), 1-2 secondary keywords, and 1-2 long-tail variations.
- Think about what a startup founder would actually type into Google or ask an AI assistant.
- Example: primary "SaaS landing page design", secondary "startup landing page mistakes", long-tail "why do SaaS landing pages have low conversion rates"

---

## FAQs (3-5)

Generate 3-5 FAQ pairs based on the article content. These serve two purposes: they can be added to the blog post itself for reader value, and they help with passage indexing and AI citation.

- Each question should be something a real founder would search or ask an AI.
- Each answer should be 2-4 sentences, direct, and self-contained (it should make sense even without reading the full article).
- Use David's voice in the answers — direct, specific, no filler.
- Don't use generic questions like "Why is this important?" — make them specific to the topic.
- Front-load the direct answer in the first sentence, then add context.

**Example:**
```
Q: How much does a design retainer cost compared to hiring in-house?
A: A design retainer runs $5,000-$8,000/mo for senior-level work. A full-time in-house designer costs $80,000-$140,000/yr in salary alone, plus benefits, equipment, and 2-3 months of onboarding before they're productive. The retainer gives you comparable output at roughly half the cost with zero hiring risk.
```

## E-E-A-T signals to bake into the content

Google's quality raters assess Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, and Trustworthiness. David's content naturally scores well here when written properly:

- **Experience:** First-person accounts from real client work (without fabricating specific stories). "I see this pattern across most of the startups I work with" counts.
- **Expertise:** Use design-specific terminology correctly. Reference specific tools (Webflow, Figma), metrics (conversion rates, MRR), and frameworks (design audits, 72-hour V1).
- **Authoritativeness:** Link to Humbl Design's work, reference the 50+ founders served, mention real proof points.
- **Trustworthiness:** Author attribution (David Pokorny, Humbl Design), publication date, specific claims backed by experience.

---

## AI citation readiness

AI search engines (ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google AI Mode) are becoming major traffic sources. To optimize for AI citation:

- Write clear, quotable statements with specific data points. AI systems prefer extracting concrete facts over vague claims.
- Use strong heading hierarchy (H1 → H2 → H3) so AI can parse the content structure.
- Front-load the direct answer in the first 40-50 words of each major section — passage indexing ranks these blocks independently.
- Include structured data where relevant (tables, numbered steps, comparison lists).
